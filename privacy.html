Tot Toolbox privacy policy

Last updated October 31, 2026

This policy covers this website, Tot Toolbox and the apps it offers: Tot Flash Cards, Tot Counting, Tot Compare and Tot Routines. Bumplings and Tot Potty have their own policies (Bumplings, Tot Potty). All of them are made by Danford Enterprises, LLC, doing business as Tot Toolbox.

None of our apps have ads, analytics, tracking or accounts. We do not collect, sell or share personal information, and we never collect anything from children.

Tot Flash Cards, Tot Counting and Tot Compare

These apps do not use the internet. Settings stay on the phone. Tot Flash Cards has a grown-up mode for recording the words in your own voice; it asks for the microphone only then, and the recordings stay on the phone. The copy from Google Play has no such mode and no microphone permission.

Tot Routines

What it keeps, on your phone. What you enter about your children: their names, birthdates and photos, their interests, school days and times and who takes them, another home's schedule, when they woke and slept, medicine given and when, their routines, day plans and what's been done; the photos and recordings of your voice you add to routine steps; and the family journal's notes, photos and videos. If you share, also the first name you give so the others see who did what. It's kept in the app's own storage on your phone. Nothing is sent to us, and the app has no ads, analytics or accounts.

Camera, microphone and notifications. The camera takes step photos, journal photos and videos and your child's photo, and scans the invite code another grown-up's phone shows. The microphone records your voice for a step, only when you tap Record. Notifications remind you of routines (set on your phone) and, while sharing, tell you what the other grown-ups added. Photos and videos you choose to save to your gallery are copied there, outside the app, like any other photo.

If you share with another grown-up's phone (it's off unless you turn it on): the children, routines, day plans, sleep times, medicine, ticks and journal (its notes, photos and videos, who has seen them, and each child's photo) are encrypted on your phone, with a key only your family's phones have, before they're sent. This is end-to-end encryption: our sync server (sync.tottoolbox.com) keeps and passes on only the encrypted copy, and we cannot read it. Photos and videos wait on the server, encrypted, only until your family's other phones have collected them, and are deleted then, or after 48 hours at most. Step photos and voice recordings are never shared. While the app is closed, it checks the server every so often for your family's news. The server sees what any server does: your IP address (used only to limit abuse, and not kept with your data), and when your phones sync and how much they send, but not what it is. Each phone in your family is listed in the app, and any of them can be removed; the family then gets a new key, so the removed phone can't read anything after that.

Supporting us (paid, optional). A family can support Tot Toolbox by the month or the year, and its journal videos are then shared at the best quality its phones can make (up to 4K). This is offered only in the copy of the app from tottoolbox.com. The copy from Google Play sells nothing and takes no payment details; it only asks our server, with the random key below, whether the family has a plan. Payment is taken by Stripe, on Stripe's own page; Stripe receives your card details and email address under its privacy policy, and we never see your card. Our server keeps, for each plan: Stripe's ids for the subscription and its customer, which plan it is and when it ends. It's filed under a random key the app makes, not under your family or anything in the app, so the server can't tell whose family it is.

Backups. Like most apps, Tot Routines' storage is included in your phone's own backups (such as Google's backup of your phone, to your Google account). While you share, everything the app keeps is locked with a key that stays on your phone, so a backup holds only an encrypted copy. You can turn the same lock on without sharing, in Photos and privacy.

On the web. Tot Routines also runs in a web browser (tottoolbox.com/routines/app/), for iPhones. It works the same way: what you enter is kept in that browser's own storage on your phone, and nothing is sent to us. Sharing is encrypted on your phone in just the same way, so our sync server still can't read it. There, the lock's key is kept in the browser beside what it locks, and step photos and voice recordings, reminders and the check for news while it's closed aren't part of it. The web page adds no cookies, analytics or third-party scripts.

With Tot Potty on the same phone, each child's day shows how potty training is going, read on the phone from Tot Potty (see its policy). With Bumplings on the same phone, Tot Routines' family page shows the baby on the way, read on the phone from Bumplings each time it's shown: the pregnancy's dates, appointments, checklist, how many journal entries there are, the newest bump photo and, gently, how the journal and the latest mood check have gone (see Bumplings' policy for exactly what). Tot Routines doesn't save any of it or send it anywhere, including to another grown-up's phone it shares routines with.

Tot Toolbox

Tot Toolbox downloads the list of apps and the apps themselves from tottoolbox.com. It sends nothing about you or your phone. It checks which of our apps are installed on your phone, and that stays on the phone. Our website host sees your IP address, as any website does, to deliver the files. The copy from Google Play downloads only the list: it opens each app's page on Google Play, and Google's own policies cover what happens there.

This website

tottoolbox.com's pages use Google Analytics, so we can see how the site is used: pages visited, how people found us, the kind of device and roughly where (city level). Google sets cookies to tell a returning visitor from a new one, and Google's own policy covers how it uses that data (How Google uses information from sites that use its services). You can opt out with Google's opt-out browser add-on or your browser's tracking protection. Google Analytics isn't on this privacy policy, the apps, or the apps' web versions (such as tottoolbox.com/routines/app/). The site has no ads and no accounts.

Separately, each day we count page views by site, page path and the referring website's hostname. There is no cookie, no IP address stored, no user agent and no per-visitor id. The same count covers taps on a few buttons, such as a download or print button, by the button's name and nothing more. The visitor's address is held in memory for up to an hour only to limit abuse and is never written to disk. Daily counts are kept, to compare one year with the next. The count goes to our own server, sync.tottoolbox.com.

This count is only for the website's pages. The apps, and their web versions, don't send it, and they still collect nothing new.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling an app deletes everything it stored on your phone (a copy in your phone's backups stays until that backup is replaced or deleted). Tot Routines' encrypted shared copy is deleted from our server when sharing is stopped on the last phone, or at once with "Stop sharing on every phone and delete the shared copy" in the app's sharing screen. A shared copy no phone has used for a year is deleted automatically. If you can't use the app to do it, email support@tottoolbox.com; since we can't read the encrypted copies or tell whose is whose, we'll help you delete it from the app.

Children

The toddler apps are made for young children to use with a grown-up. They collect no personal information from children and contain no ads, links out of the app or purchases. Grown-up settings are behind a press-and-hold.

Tot Routines is for parents and other grown-ups, not for children to use on their own (a child only follows a picture chart a grown-up shows them). What it keeps about your children is entered by you, stays on your phone, and is only ever shared, encrypted, with the phones in your family that you invite. We never receive it in a form we can read.
